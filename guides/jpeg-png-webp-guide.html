JPEG vs PNG vs WebP: Image Format and Compression Guide

Compare JPEG, PNG and WebP for photos, logos and websites. Learn how quality, transparency, dimensions and browser-based compression affect image size.

JPEG for photographs

JPEG is a common choice for photographs and detailed images. It normally uses lossy compression to trade some fine detail for a smaller file. Reducing quality too much can cause blockiness and blurred lettering. JPEG cannot retain a transparent background.

PNG for sharp graphics

PNG uses lossless image compression and supports transparent backgrounds. It often works well for logos, line art, diagrams and screenshots with small text. For complex photos, a PNG file can be much larger than a JPEG. A JPEG-like quality slider does not normally make PNG lossy.

WebP for web delivery

WebP supports lossy and lossless image compression and can preserve transparency. It can be a useful choice for website photographs and graphics, though the result depends on the source image and compression settings. Confirm that the apps you will send images to support WebP.

Image dimensions are important

An image that is 4000 pixels across may be excessive when displayed as a 600-pixel thumbnail. Resizing to suitable dimensions can reduce total bytes before changing compression quality. If you plan to print a large photograph, save the original resolution separately.

Not every conversion reduces size

A highly compressed input can sometimes become larger after conversion. Compare the exported file size and inspect edges, gradients and writing at its expected display size. Choose the combination that preserves the detail you actually need.

Keep an original copy

Our browser tool converts images locally with a canvas, without uploading the photo to our server. Export can remove metadata such as EXIF details, and especially large photos may exceed browser memory. Preserve the untouched original if you need print quality, metadata or later edits.

دليل الفرق بين JPEG وPNG وWebP عند ضغط الصور

JPEG مناسب غالباً للصور الفوتوغرافية، ويقلل الحجم مقابل فقد بعض التفاصيل، ولا يحتفظ بالخلفية الشفافة.

PNG مناسب للشعارات والنصوص داخل الصور ولقطات الشاشة، ويحافظ على الشفافية ويستخدم ضغطاً غير فاقد.

WebP مناسب للمواقع وقد يقدم حجماً أقل مع دعم الشفافية، لكن يجب التأكد من توافق البرامج التي تستقبل الصورة.

تقليل أبعاد الصور يحسن الحجم أحياناً أكثر من خفض الجودة. احتفظ بالملف الأصلي وتحقق من النتيجة.
